One last thing, because it matters more than the headline.

Sixty-three percent of citations arriving after retraction sounds devastating, and it is partly an artefact. The median paper here spent two years valid and four years retracted. It simply had longer.

So divide by the time available. Every dot is one paper: how fast it was cited while it was valid, against how fast it has been cited since.

The diagonal is where nothing changed. Most papers sit below it, and the median lands at zero point six two — about a third off the rate.

But sixty-three of the two hundred and five sit above the line. Cited faster dead than alive.

Retraction slows the spread by about a third. It does not stop it.

That is the number we will defend.
