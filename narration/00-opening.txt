A retraction is one of the few moments where science says, formally and with a date on it, that something is no longer knowledge.

The paper is withdrawn. The record is corrected. That is supposed to be the end of it.

We took three hundred retracted papers and counted every citation they have ever received — a hundred and forty-four thousand of them — and sorted each one by whether it arrived before the retraction, or after.

Ninety-one thousand arrived after.
